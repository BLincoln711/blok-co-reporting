The Steve Clark Team is a founding Compass Indiana team, an IBJ Top 25 All-Star Agent Team and a RealTrends-ranked team. That is real, earned authority. None of it is reaching an AI search engine, because there is almost nothing on the site for an engine to read.

Eighteen community pages are live for Carmel, Zionsville, Westfield, Fishers, Noblesville, Whitestown, Geist, Williams Creek, Meridian-Kessler, Meridian Hills, Broad Ripple, Brownsburg, McCordsville, Fortville, Lawrence, Pike, Downtown Indy and Morris. Their combined original content is 1,218 words, an average of 68 words per community. Williams Creek, the most exclusive address in the footprint, gets 54 words. Every one of those pages then hands the rest of its screen to an IDX feed.

The clearest single finding: across those 18 pages, steveclarkteam.com serves 87 listing cards branded F.C. Tucker Company and 38 branded Compass Indiana. The team's own website is doing more than twice as much brand-building for its largest competitor as it is for its own brokerage.

This is not a criticism of the site build. It is the normal outcome of an IDX-first template that was never given editorial content to balance it. It is also the entire opportunity: the pages already exist, they already rank for the right place names, and they are one content layer away from being the best answer in the market.

What is already right

The URL footprint is excellent. Eighteen clean community slugs, a Buyers page, a Sellers page, Compass Concierge, Bridge Loan Services, a home valuation tool, seven agent bio pages and a Meet the Team page. Baseline Place, Organization and PostalAddress schema is already firing on the community pages. The structure is sound. It is the content inside it that does not exist.

What is blocking citation

No FAQ schema anywhere on the site. No data tables. One H2 per community page. A blog that has published a single placeholder post since July 2020. AI engines cite passages that directly answer a question, carry structured markup and sit next to original data. The site currently offers none of those three.

The Architecture

Nine pillars, 98 spokes, and a 9-page entity layer underneath all of it.

Each pillar is a definitive hub page. Spokes link up to their pillar and laterally to each other where a buyer would genuinely move between the two questions. The schema mix leans on FAQPage, Place, RealEstateAgent, HowTo and Dataset, the structured types that generative engines preferentially quote.

How One Pillar Works

Pillar 3 shown in full, because it is the fastest path to inbound leads.

Eli Lilly has committed more than 13 billion dollars to its Lebanon campus. Meta broke ground in February 2026 on a 10 billion dollar, 1,500-acre data center. Together that is more capital arriving in one Indiana community than in any other in the state's history, on an IEDC land assembly of roughly 9,000 acres, with a new I-65 interchange and a mixed-use village that went out for master-developer proposals in July 2026. At full build the district is projected to employ tens of thousands of people.

Every one of those people has to live somewhere, and almost all of them will ask an AI assistant where before they ask an agent. Right now no team in Central Indiana owns that answer.

The honest read

The Steve Clark Team cannot out-publish The Carrie Holle Group from a standing start. She has years of a head start and a real library. Trying to win on volume is the wrong game and it would take eighteen months.

The winnable game is structure. Not one competitor in this market has a single FAQ schema block or a single original data table. Answer-first formatting, FAQ markup and dated proprietary data are what generative engines actually quote, and they are cheap to add to content that is being written anyway. That is where a team with better bones and no content beats a team with more content and no structure.

Second honest note: the IDX template is a structural problem the content program cannot fix on its own. As long as a community page pairs 68 words with 24 competitor listing cards, the page reads as a listing feed, not a resource. Rebalancing that template is a prerequisite, not a nice-to-have.

What We Need From You in This Session

The intake questionnaire did not reach us. These eight answers unblock the build. Everything above was assembled from the public record, so treat it as a first draft to correct, not a finished plan.

Question 01 · blocks the entity layer

Which production numbers do we publish everywhere?

steveclarkteam.com says 115 million dollars over the last two years and 1,200-plus families. The Compass profile says 500 million dollars in career sales and 1,500-plus families. Both may be true measured differently, but AI engines cross-check, and an inconsistency here weakens every citation. We need one set, stated one way, everywhere.

Question 02 · blocks nine author pages

Who owns which lane, and who stays out of public content?

Steve, Jeff, Beth, Tonya, Angelo, Lauren, Jackie and Samantha. Engines cite named people with a defined specialty far more readily than a team byline. We need a lane per person, confirmation of who can be quoted, and a note on anyone who should not be named publicly.

Question 03 · sets pillar weighting

Buyer or seller weighted, and which price bands?

This decides whether Pillar 6 or Pillar 7 gets built out first, and whether the community guides lead with the 400 thousand dollar tier or the estate tier.

Question 04 · sizes the biggest differentiator

How much of the business is new construction, and how hard do we lean in?

Pillar 2 is the strongest opening in this market because no competitor writes about builder negotiation honestly. It is also the one place where naming builders has commercial consequences. Tell us how direct to be, and which builder relationships are off limits.

Question 05 · sequences 18 town guides

Which communities are primary, which are secondary, which are IDX only?

Eighteen pages exist. We do not think all eighteen deserve a full guide in year one. Our working assumption is Carmel, Zionsville, Westfield, Fishers, Noblesville, Whitestown and Geist as primary. Correct us.

Question 06 · defines Tier 3

Which subdivisions actually matter?

Central Indiana buyers search by subdivision name, and a competitor already has pages on Village of WestClay, Jackson's Grant, Bridlebourne and Laurelwood. We need your list of the 20 to 25 that carry the most volume and the most of your business.

Question 07 · in or out

Compass Concierge, bridge loans and Private Exclusives: content or not?

These are real searched questions with real intent, and you already have pages for them. We want to write them honestly, which means covering cost and tradeoffs. Confirm that is acceptable within Compass brand requirements, or tell us to leave them out.

Question 08 · blocks publishing entirely

Who controls the site, the analytics and the IDX template?

We need WordPress admin, Google Analytics and Google Search Console access, and we need to know who can change the community page template. The template rebalance in the honest-read section above requires whoever built the site.

Also useful, but not blocking

Feeder markets you see most often. Any employer or relocation-department relationships worth naming. Which designations each team member holds. Fair Housing and Compass compliance language you want enforced. Competitors you specifically want to outrank. Seasonality in your market. Anything you do not want us writing about.
